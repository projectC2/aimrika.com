The problem

Readiness has become a software problem — and you cannot fly your way to trustworthy AI.

Validating autonomous flight in UK and EU airspace is structurally constrained, and the constraint is getting worse. The systems that most need to be flown thousands of times to be trusted are the systems that are hardest to fly even once.

01 A first flight costs months

Even a single initial test flight requires regulatory approval and certified equipment that routinely takes months to obtain.

02 The envelope forbids autonomy

Lawful flight today demands a trained operator, manual control, and visual line of sight — precisely what makes a system not autonomous.

03 No pathway for on-board AI

There is no mature regulatory or practical route to testing genuine on-board AI decision-making in real airspace. The rules were not written for a pilot made of software.

04 Physical AI is hungry

A robust agent must learn the common sense of the world across thousands of flight hours of training and adversarial iteration — operationally impossible under the constraints above.

The solution · our flagship

The Range — where physical AI earns its wings.

We move the flight hours into simulation and keep only the irreducible minimum in the sky. A high-fidelity synthetic proving ground that runs thousands of autonomous flights a day — and the same agent that passes the Range runs unchanged on the real edge hardware aboard the real airframe.

STEP 01

Train

The agent learns to fly, perceive, navigate, recover and survive across weather, terrain, sensor noise and induced failures.

STEP 02

Rehearse

The exact mission — geometry, target, environment, threats — is flown to standard. A true dry run, no permit, no risk.

STEP 03

Coordinate

Aircraft, vehicles and vessels learn to operate as a team — the multi-agent rehearsal live ranges can rarely afford.

STEP 04

Certify

Every behaviour is benchmarked against a reference autopilot on identical geometry and physics — the etalon.

STEP 05

Execute

The certified agent deploys to the real airframe. Real flight confirms; it does not discover.

The etalon method

Prove, don't assert.

No capability is "done" until it beats a control, repeatably. A mature reference autopilot flies the task on identical geometry under identical physics; its track is the achievable ceiling. The agent then flies the same task — and because everything except who is flying is held constant, every difference is attributable purely to the agent.

  • Simulation is the authority, not the demo. The Range is where autonomy is built and certified; real flight confirms it.
  • Single source of truth. Every behaviour, benchmark and decision is traceable — trust is earned by auditability.
  • Sovereign and honest. We build capability democracies own and can scrutinise; we never ship what we have not measured.
